# RFC 0001: Open Questions for Community Comment

Companion to [RFC 0001](rfc-0001.md). These topics have been identified but not yet developed. Comments are welcome: see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## 1. AI in qualitative scenario work

AI can write the qualitative storylines that underlie many quantitative scenarios: hundreds, perhaps thousands of them. This is timely, as the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways are being evaluated and updated through the Scenario Evolution Process [SEP]. Is this within the scope of RFC 0001, or related to it? What guidance is needed?

## 2. Synthetic surveys

Scenario research uses surveys of experts and of the public to check scenarios. AI agents can now answer such surveys ("synthetic surveys"). What guidance is needed?

## 3. AI use by scenario users

Scenario users, such as financial institutions, municipalities and corporations, may use AI to process, interpret and visualize scenarios. Rather than guide their AI use, what should scenario producers communicate with their data (for example, labels, intended uses and limitations) so that this context survives AI processing?

## 4. Wider societal discussion

- **Key principles.** Should the principles (RFC 0001, Section 1.3) include equity or justice, or is this for the IAMC Scientific Working Group on Justice, Equity and Human Development (JED)?
- **Broader participation.** How should the discussion be widened to other fields, such as sociology of technology and the social sciences, and to scenario users and policymakers? Precedents include the 1975 Asilomar conference on recombinant DNA, whose roughly 140 participants included lawyers, journalists and government officials [BERG2008], and the IPCC workshop on the use of scenarios (Bangkok, 2023) [IPCC-BKK2023].

## 5. Terminology

RFC 0001 distinguishes AI-generated from human-modeled scenarios (Section 2.2). IPCC Working Group I uses "emulator" for simple models such as MAGICC and FaIR, distinguished from CMIP Earth system models [AR6-WG1-CH7]. Should RFC 0001 align its terminology?

## 6. Related guidance

Related guidance includes the COPE position on authorship and AI tools [COPE], the ICMJE recommendations [ICMJE], the Leiden Declaration [LEIDEN] and the European Commission living guidelines on the responsible use of generative AI in research [EC-LIVING]. The last is the most closely related to RFC 0001: it is also a living document on responsible use, and it shares the emphasis on accountability, confidentiality, and transparency and disclosure. RFC 0001 should align with it.
